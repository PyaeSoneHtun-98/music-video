# Music video story outline — The Light We Kept

**Working runtime:** 6:50 (410 seconds)  
**Working song:** the local 6:50 audio file of RADWIMPS’ “Sparkle,” for a mainly private video shared with close friends.  
**Production grid:** 27 clips of up to 15 seconds, plus a 5-second ending = 28 story sheets.  
**Status:** Story and shot ideas only. Timings are provisional until the exact audio is aligned in an editor.
**On-screen text:** None. Character names are production labels only; no name cards or title cards appear in the finished video.

## Characters and visual anchors

- **Zin Zin:** round gold glasses, side braid with red tie, blue patterned top, wide-leg jeans, brown shoulder bag with spotted plush charm, teal dinosaur plush. Cheerful and expressive.
- **Pyae Sone:** short dark hair, black glasses, white short-sleeve shirt, dark trousers, empty hands. Calm and thoughtful. Use Pyae Sone as his name even though the character-sheet image contains a different printed title. Do not show him holding an extra shirt, jacket, or other clothing.
- Keep the outfits and accessories consistent. Use the teal plush, spotted bag charm, and reflections as recurring visual cues.

## Story

Years after a happy summer in a coastal town, Zin Zin and Pyae Sone return on the same evening to keep a promise: meet at the lighthouse overlook after the rain. A storm interrupts transit and darkens the town. Each leaves the meeting place to search for the other, repeatedly passing through places they once shared. Zin Zin loses her small bag charm; Pyae Sone finds it and realizes she has gone toward the old lighthouse path. They climb from different sides as the weather clears. At first light they finally see each other, return the charm, and watch the city brighten together.

The drama is emotional and visual: waiting, uncertainty, memories, near misses, recognition, and relief. There is no fantasy event or borrowed film plot. The sparkle comes from rain, wet pavement, sea light, passing trains, festival lanterns, and the first clear sky.

## Visual progression

1. **Warm memory:** soft gold, open compositions, gentle movement.
2. **Separation:** rain blue, reflections and frames within frames; avoid showing both characters together in the present.
3. **Search:** energetic motion, strong diagonals, alternating routes, wider geography.
4. **Reunion:** blue hour into sunrise; first clean two-person frame, then close details and a final wide image.

## Story sheet format for the next pass

For each 15-second target, create a numbered contact sheet like the supplied example: 4–6 key frames, exact time spans, shot size, camera move, character action, lighting, background continuity, and a short Seedance prompt. Set every final video clip to 16:9 landscape and compose its actual input/reference still at 16:9. A contact sheet is a planning aid; its panel rectangles do not set the video aspect ratio. The existing S01 contact sheets have narrower panel rectangles, so do not feed them directly to Seedance as shot references. Use separate 16:9 frames for actual generation. Use image references from the two character sheets; do not include the text labels from those sheets in generated scenes.
